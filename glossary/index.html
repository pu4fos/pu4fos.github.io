Amateur Radio Glossary

Q-codes, on-air abbreviations and technical terms every operator meets, defined plainly. Terms are grouped; use your browser's find (Ctrl/⌘-F) to jump to one.

Q-codes

TermMeaning
QRZ?Who is calling me?
QRMMan-made interference from other stations.
QRNNatural/atmospheric noise (static).
QRPLow transmit power (typically ≤5 W CW / 10 W SSB).
QROHigh transmit power.
QRTI am ceasing transmission / closing the station.
QRVI am ready (to receive/operate).
QRXPlease wait / stand by.
QSBFading — the signal is varying in strength.
QSLI acknowledge receipt; also a card confirming a contact.
QSOA two-way radio contact/conversation.
QSYChange to another frequency.
QTHStation location.
QRGExact frequency of a station.
QSKFull break-in: hearing between CW elements.

On-air abbreviations

TermMeaning
73Best regards (sign-off).
88Love and kisses (affectionate sign-off).
CQGeneral call to any station.
DXA distant or rare station/contact.
DE'From' — precedes the sender's callsign.
RSTSignal report: Readability, Strength, Tone.
SWLShortwave listener (receive-only hobbyist).
YLYoung lady — a female operator.
OMOld man — a male operator (friendly).
XYLAn operator's wife.
TNX / TUThanks / thank you.
PSEPlease.
AGNAgain — please repeat.
WXWeather.
HI HILaughter in Morse/on air.

Operating & procedure

TermMeaning
ElmerAn experienced ham who mentors newcomers.
RagchewA long, casual conversation on air.
SimplexTransmit and receive on the same frequency.
DuplexTransmit and receive on different frequencies.
OffsetFrequency shift between repeater input and output.
RepeaterA station that receives and retransmits to extend range.
CTCSS / PLSub-audible tone that opens a repeater's squelch.
PTTPush-to-talk — the transmit switch.
NetA scheduled on-air meeting of stations.
QSL cardA physical/electronic card confirming a contact.
LoTWARRL's Logbook of The World, an online QSL system.
ContestA timed competition to make the most contacts.
DXpeditionA trip to activate a rare location on the air.

Technical & equipment

TermMeaning
SWR / VSWRStanding Wave Ratio — how well an antenna is matched.
ImpedanceOpposition to AC/RF, measured in ohms (usually 50 Ω).
ResonanceThe frequency at which an antenna is electrically 'tuned'.
dBDecibel — a logarithmic ratio of power or voltage.
dBi / dBdAntenna gain vs. an isotropic / a dipole reference.
ERP / EIRPEffective radiated power (vs. dipole / isotropic).
BalunDevice matching a balanced antenna to unbalanced coax.
Coax / feedlineCable carrying RF between radio and antenna.
DipoleA half-wave antenna fed at the center.
YagiA directional beam antenna with driven and parasitic elements.
Ground planeA vertical antenna with radial 'ground' elements.
HarmonicAn unwanted signal at a multiple of the operating frequency.
S-meter / S-unitReceiver signal-strength scale (S1–S9+).
SquelchMutes the receiver until a signal exceeds a threshold.
APRSAutomatic Packet Reporting System (position/data over RF).

Modes

TermMeaning
CWContinuous Wave — Morse-code telegraphy.
AMAmplitude Modulation voice mode.
FMFrequency Modulation, common on VHF/UHF.
SSB (USB/LSB)Single sideband voice — efficient HF mode.
RTTYRadioteletype — classic FSK text mode.
PSK31A keyboard-to-keyboard narrow digital mode.
FT8A weak-signal digital mode for tough conditions.

Bands & propagation

TermMeaning
HFHigh Frequency, 3–30 MHz — long-distance skywave.
VHFVery High Frequency, 30–300 MHz — mostly line of sight.
UHFUltra High Frequency, 300 MHz–3 GHz.
IonosphereCharged upper atmosphere that refracts HF signals.
Skip / hopLong-distance signal reflected by the ionosphere.
Gray lineEnhanced propagation along the day/night terminator.
Sporadic EBrief VHF openings via patches of ionized E-layer.
NVISNear-Vertical Incidence Skywave for regional HF coverage.
Maidenhead locatorGrid-square coordinate system (e.g., GH80ae).

Satellites

TermMeaning
DopplerFrequency shift caused by a satellite's motion.
Uplink / downlinkFrequencies you transmit to / receive from a satellite.
TransponderSatellite subsystem that relays a band of signals.
AOS / LOSAcquisition / Loss of Signal at a pass's start / end.
BeaconAn automatic transmitter marking presence/propagation.
