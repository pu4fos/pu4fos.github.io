Modes

A mode is how information is impressed on the carrier wave. Each trades bandwidth, complexity and robustness differently, and part of the craft is picking the right one for the conditions.

CW — Morse code

CW (Continuous Wave) switches a carrier on and off as Morse. It occupies almost no bandwidth and punches through noise better than voice, so a few watts of CW can cross an ocean when SSB cannot. It remains beloved for its efficiency and simplicity.

Voice: AM, SSB and FM

AM is the oldest voice mode, now niche. SSB (single sideband) strips away the carrier and one sideband, concentrating all power in the voice — the workhorse of HF DX. FM gives clean, quiet audio and rules VHF/UHF repeaters and local simplex, at the cost of wider bandwidth and a sharp drop-off when signals get weak.

Digital modes

Computers opened a new era. RTTY and PSK31 send text keyboard-to-keyboard. FT8 and its relatives use clever coding to decode signals buried below the noise floor, making worldwide contacts possible with tiny power and compromise antennas — one reason amateur radio keeps growing.
