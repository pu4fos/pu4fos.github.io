Antennas

The antenna is the most important part of any station. A good antenna with a modest radio beats an expensive radio with a poor antenna every time — it is where your signal actually becomes a radio wave.

The half-wave dipole

The dipole is the reference antenna: a wire cut to about half a wavelength, fed in the middle. Its length in metres is roughly 143 ÷ frequency (MHz). Simple, cheap and effective, it radiates best broadside to the wire and is the honest starting point for understanding every other antenna.

Verticals and beams

A vertical radiates equally in all horizontal directions (omnidirectional) and needs a ground or radials; it is popular where space is tight and for low-angle DX. A Yagi beam adds parasitic elements to focus energy in one direction, giving gain and rejecting noise from other directions — at the cost of size and a rotator.

Gain, polarization and height

Gain is not free power; it is focusing — concentrating radiation where you want it, measured in dBi or dBd. Polarization (vertical or horizontal) should usually match between stations on VHF/UHF. And height matters enormously: raising an antenna clears obstacles and lowers the radiation angle, often doing more for your signal than more power ever could.
