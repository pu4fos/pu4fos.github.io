About PU4FOS

My name is Fernando. I am a programmer, a wood-carver and an amateur-radio operator, among other things that curiosity kept adding along the way. I work with technology, but I also like to get my hands into the material itself: wood, tools, wires, circuits, machines. I have this taste for building, taking apart, experimenting and finding out how things work. Amateur radio ended up gathering much of those inclinations in one place. I operate from Belo Horizonte, Minas Gerais, with the callsign PU4FOS.

My relationship with radio was born far more from curiosity than from any wish to simply talk over a distance. I was always drawn to understanding what lies behind things: how a signal manages to cross kilometres, how an antenna turns current into a wave, why one frequency behaves one way and another differently, what is really happening when someone, on the other side, answers my call.

That is how amateur radio stopped being merely an activity and became a particular way of observing the world.

I like to experiment. To test antennas, measure, take apart, modify, compare results, look for the best possible setup within the limits I have. Radio, for me, has a very technical side, but it does not end there. There is also the pleasure of making a difficult contact, following a satellite passing overhead, correcting Doppler during a QSO, hearing a station emerge from the noise, or simply spending a few minutes listening to a frequency without knowing exactly what will happen.

I have a special interest in VHF and UHF communications and in the possibilities that appear when you combine knowledge, improvisation and a little persistence. Satellites, antennas, propagation, portable equipment and bench experiments are part of this universe I am always exploring.

I also like what amateur radio teaches without needing to announce that it is teaching: patience, attention, discipline, respect for the spectrum and, above all, the ability to listen. After all, communication does not begin when we press the PTT. It begins when we pay attention.

Perhaps that is exactly the part I like most about radio. It is technology, but it is also encounter. You can spend a whole night tinkering with equipment, adjusting an antenna or chasing an almost imperceptible signal and, suddenly, discover that there is someone hundreds or thousands of kilometres away doing exactly the same thing.

This site was born to record a bit of that path: experiences, contacts, equipment, antennas, satellites, tests, discoveries, mistakes and successes. Not as a definitive manual — because in amateur radio there is always something new to learn — but as a kind of open field notebook.

PU4FOS is my place in this immense network of radio amateurs. A small callsign among many others, but enough to turn a frequency, a burst of noise or a few received characters into a story.

And perhaps that is what keeps fascinating me about radio: in the end, we are using machines to do something essentially human — trying to reach someone who is far away and say: I am here.
